Architecture

Lea Mouawad is an architect and immersive experience designer whose work sits at the intersection of architecture, storytelling, and emerging media.

Born in Lebanon and based in Paris, she creates environments that shape how people perceive, navigate, and emotionally connect with space. Her projects range from architecture and interior design to immersive installations and digital worlds, united by a common ambition: transforming space into experience.

Rather than viewing architecture as a static object, she approaches it as a narrative framework — one that guides movement, influences emotion, and reveals stories through exploration.

Her practice is driven by a simple question:
What does a space make us feel?
The answer becomes the foundation of every project she creates.

01 / Architecture

Neighbourhood
Swimming Pool

Beirut, Lebanon — Architectural Design

Kinetic Architecture Parametric Design Civic Infrastructure Rhino + Grasshopper

The design translates the fluid, rhythmic movement of swimmers into an architectural expression overhead. The kinetic roof is composed of panels that shift, rotate, and fold in programmed sequences — a canopy that mirrors the bodies moving beneath it.

As swimmers move through lanes below, the structure responds: opening to flood the pool with natural light during peak hours, folding inward to create sheltered microclimates at the edges, cycling through ventilation states throughout the day.

The neighbourhood pool is reimagined not as a static vessel but as a civic machine in constant, gentle motion — tuned to human rhythm.

02 / Architecture

Design
Museum

Beirut, Lebanon — Cultural Architecture

Cultural Architecture Adaptive Facade Museum Design Beirut

The Design Museum proposes a building skin that refuses to stay fixed. The facade is composed of layered, repositionable panels that shift configuration throughout the day — changing the visual character and porosity of the structure in response to light, programme, and season.

At dawn the museum reads as a dense, tactile mass. By midday panels open into a lattice of shadow and air. At dusk the surface catches and fragments the last light, dissolving into an almost immaterial screen.

Conceptual Diagram

01 02 03 04 05 06
  1. Point distribution — structural anchor points placed across the base plane.
  2. Vertical extrusion — elements rise from each anchor point, defining the building's skeleton.
  3. Articulation — elements begin to bend and curve in response to wind and programme forces.
  4. Structural branching — elements develop into an organic, interconnected network.
  5. Material layering — coloured facade panels are threaded through the structure.
  6. Final facade — the building enclosed, the metamorphic skin fully resolved.

06 / Digital Sculpture

ROOM

Digital Sculpture Series — Abhominal

Digital Sculpture Posthumanism Biotechnology 3ds Max + ZBrush + Keyshot

Abhominal — an archaic word meaning inhuman — is an exploratory blog of the human form. These digital sculptures fuse abstract geometric, architectural, and biological shapes to depict a dark evolutionary future: one where biotechnology is used to engineer "perfect" beings.

Visible and mobile, the body is a thing among things. It is caught in the fabric of the world, and its coherence is that of a thing. But because it moves and sees, it holds things in a circle around itself. Things are an annex or an extension of itself — embedded in its flesh, part of its complete definition. The world is made of the same substance as the body.
— Maurice Merleau-Ponty

Concept — The Fusion

Each sculpture is born from the collision of three sources: an organic reference — the skin, as painted surface and living membrane — a structural reference — the wheel as load-bearing geometry — and the computer as medium, merging both into a single hybrid entity. The result is neither purely biological nor purely mechanical, but something that exists in between.

05 / Conceptual

RUBIK

Conceptual Project

Parametric Design Conceptual Architecture Spatial Logic 3ds Max + Keyshot

The Rubik's Cube is more than a puzzle — it is a model for critical reasoning. No matter how scrambled, any configuration of the cube can be resolved back into order. The same logic applies to language, philosophy, and ultimately, to space: apparent complexity is never truly irreducible. Beneath the chaos lies a system.

With approximately 43 quintillion possible combinations, the cube was once an open question — could a human even solve it? The answer revealed something fundamental: every paradox, every apparent dead end, is simply a matter of untangling the language to expose the underlying rule. The law of identity — A is A — is the architecture of all reasoning. Understanding it is like seeing the cube in its fully solved state.

This project begins from that premise. The Rubik's Cube becomes the generative logic for a spatial system — one governed by rotation, adjacency, and transformation, where shifting one element changes the state of every connected element.

The Parametrics of the Rubik

Reinventing the Parametrics of this Game

Parametric Extrusion — Form Generation

The cube's rotation and stacking rules are encoded as parametric operations — each set of parameters generates a distinct formal output. Shifting a single variable cascades through the entire system, producing a new configuration. The three generated forms (136, 137, 138) demonstrate this range.

Resulting Landscape

When applied at scale, the parametric logic ceases to describe an object and begins to describe a terrain. Forms accumulate, overlap, and interlock — producing a continuous landscape whose complexity emerges from the same simple rules.

04 / Architecture

Multi-Comfort
Residence

Dubai, UAE

Residential Urban Typology Sustainable Design AutoCAD + 3ds Max + Keyshot

The Multi-Comfort Residence draws its spatial logic from the traditional medina — specifically its urban fabric, where built mass and negative space interweave in a dense, organic pattern. The voids of the medina are reinterpreted and projected into three dimensions, hollowing out the building's volume into open, shaded courtyards.

Beyond its residential programme, the building volume doubles as a structural canopy over the parking below — providing shade and contributing to a more sustainable urban footprint in Dubai's climate. Traditional architectural elements are reread through a contemporary lens, producing a building rooted in the history of Arab urbanism while meeting the demands of modern living.

Conceptual Diagram

01 02 03 04
  1. Medina fabric — the traditional urban tissue of the medina: dense, irregular, and defined by its interweaving of solid and void.
  2. Solid / void — the mass (white) is read against the negative space (black), isolating the open interstitial spaces that give the medina its character.
  3. Void at multiple scales — the negative spaces are identified at two scales: large communal courtyards (red) and intimate passageways (blue), forming a spatial hierarchy.
  4. 3D projection — the 2D void pattern is extruded and folded into three dimensions, generating the residential volume and its system of shaded, carved-out spaces.

03 / Architecture

Primary
School

Beirut, Lebanon — USJ Huvelin

Education Modular Design Urban Context AutoCAD + Rhino + Keyshot

Located opposite USJ Huvelin University, this primary school serves children aged 6 to 11. The programme includes modern classrooms, a cafeteria, secretariat, library, and a playground — all unified by a single spatial logic.

The concept draws from a modular system: a single repeated unit, duplicated and stacked to generate the building's form. Classrooms share identical dimensions, making the module interchangeable — and the architecture inherently playful. Voids carved between modules bring light and movement into every level.

The pink corridor does more than link the school's wings. It bridges two cultural poles: USJ University to the west and the church to the east — threading a civic and spiritual axis directly through the heart of the building.

Conceptual Diagram

Design Reference — Jenga Identical modules, stacked and shifted. The same logic that makes Jenga structurally precarious makes this school spatially dynamic — each classroom unit is the same size, but their arrangement creates variety.
01 02 03 04
  1. Modular logic — a single classroom unit is repeated, stacked, and shifted across the site, inspired by the playful yet disciplined stacking of Jenga.
  2. Hall orientation — the central hall is positioned to open directly onto the landscape and the church, grounding the school in its urban and spiritual context.
  3. Classroom positioning — each module is angled to offer a distinct, unobstructed view, ensuring no two classrooms share the same outlook.
  4. The pink bridge — a physical and symbolic connector between USJ University (blue, west) and the church (red, east), threading a cultural axis through the school.

Game — 01

Nour

Narrative Exploration

Art Direction 3D Modelling Level Design Blender + Substance Painter + Unity

Nour is a narrative exploration game set in a Lebanon plunged into Eternal Night. Guided by a mysterious lantern, Nour searches for her mother, traverses forgotten ruins and faces menacing shadows.

Her journey becomes a quest for acceptance, carried by solidarity and collective memory.

I worked on this project as art director, 3D modeller and level designer.

This project is a dedication to Lebanon — a way to share its beauty, its history and its resilience with the world, through the language of games.

Lebanese Cultural Objects

Every asset in Nour is rooted in real Lebanese cultural objects — a way to weave the country's heritage into the world of the game.

Architectural Detail — Baalbeck Temple

The column capitals were modelled directly from the ruins of the Baalbeck Temple, capturing the intricate carvings of one of Lebanon's most iconic ancient sites.
